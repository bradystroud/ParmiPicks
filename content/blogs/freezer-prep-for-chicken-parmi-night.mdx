I freeze crumbed chicken before parmi night so I can skip the messy prep and still get a crisp base. Here’s how I pack, thaw and cook it.

## Where I stop the prep

The bit that puts me off a Tuesday night parmi is finding flour on the tap after I’ve washed up. If I’ve already got the flour, egg and crumbs out, I’d rather coat a few extra pieces of chicken and do that particular clean-up once. My stopping point is a raw, crumbed schnitzel. The sauce, ham and cheese stay out of the freezer pack.

When I reviewed [Dome Cafe in Kwinana](/reviews/dome-cafe-kwinana), I liked the crisp coating on the deep-fried chicken, but the ham was a little cold and the chips weren’t very crisp. I gave the plate 5.5/10. Those three details are worth remembering at home: the chicken, the topping and the chips can each be at a different stage of done. Freezing them separately, or leaving some of them fresh, lets me cook each bit when it needs cooking.

## Pack for the pan you actually own

Before I start, I check how many pieces my frying pan will take without their edges touching. If the answer is two, that’s my batch size. A broad, thin schnitzel looks handy until you realise you’ll have to fry four of them one at a time while somebody’s chips go cold.

I use fresh, chilled chicken breast, split the thicker pieces horizontally and gently flatten any hefty ends so each piece is roughly the same thickness. I pat the chicken dry, dust it lightly with flour, dip it in beaten egg and let the excess drip off before pressing it into dry breadcrumbs. A light coat that sticks beats a thick one that falls off in the bag. Once the chicken’s coated, I bin the flour, egg and crumbs that have been in contact with it and wash my hands, board and utensils.

## Freeze flat so you can take just one

I lay the schnitzels in a single layer on a lined metal tray that fits in the freezer. Leave a finger’s width between them and cover the tray. Once the pieces are firm, I transfer them to a freezer bag with baking paper between them, ease the air out without squashing the crumb, and mark the bag with the date and ‘raw chicken’.

That short spell on the tray earns its keep the first time only one person wants a parmi. Stack soft, freshly crumbed pieces straight into a bag and you can end up trying to prise dinner off tomorrow’s dinner. I keep the oldest bag at the front so it gets used while the coating is still in good nick.

## Thaw overnight and brown before topping

The night before, I put the pieces I need in a single layer in a covered container on the bottom shelf of the fridge. By dinner I want them flexible right through, with no hard patch in the middle. If they’re still icy, I give them more fridge time rather than leaving them out on the bench. I get the chips underway before the pan goes on; the schnitzels need my attention once they hit the oil.

I shallow-fry the thawed chicken in a little hot oil, turning it once the underside is brown and releases easily. Then I transfer it to a clean oven tray, spoon on warm tomato sauce, add ham and cheese and finish in a hot oven. Melted cheese tells me precisely one thing about dinner, and it isn’t whether the chicken’s cooked. I slide a clean probe sideways into the thickest part of the meat and look for at least 75°C before serving.

## Leave one job for parmi night

I dress the salad while the cheese melts. That’s the bit of work I’m happy to keep: the leaves are fresh, the chips are nearly ready and there’s no bowl of raw egg taking up half the sink. The freezer has saved me the fiddly part without deciding what the whole plate will taste like weeks in advance.
